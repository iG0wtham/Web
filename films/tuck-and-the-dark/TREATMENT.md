# Tuck and the Dark — treatment

**Brief.** 60 s, 1920×1080, 24 fps, Crayon Picture Book style. A little turtle who is scared of the dark.
Ages 3–6: gentle, warm narrator; short, simple words. English voice and subtitles. No user material.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The night shows its stars.** One page, one evening: the dark arrives as a wash and reveals white-wax stars that were there all along. *Rejected after reading the style's demo:* that is the demo's own peak (white-wax stars under the wash) and its ending (book on a table). Too close.
2. **A flip through bedtime pages.** Bath, pyjamas, story, lights out, one page each. Clear for small children, but four settings in 60 s leave no time to feel anything.
3. **A little night walk.** The dark falls and Tuck hides. A firefly taps on his shell and leads him on a short walk along the pond; everything she touches lights up, drawn live in lemon and orange crayon over the dark: a flower, a mushroom, a frog's lantern eyes, lily pads, then many fireflies. *The fear turns into wonder by walking into the dark, not by waiting for it to change.*

**Chosen: 3.** One subject (Tuck), one goal (to feel safe), one turn (the dark is full of little lights, and a friend shows him). The native moves are **colouring is an action** (lighting up = being coloured in) and **drawn live** (each light draws itself just ahead of Tuck), at the emotional peak, with a toy-piano note per light (counting without numbers). The wash is used once, early, for night falling.

## 2. Logline and arc

*A little turtle who hides from the dark follows a firefly and finds the dark is full of little lights.*

- **Setup (0–17 s).** A sunny pond. Tuck plays, waves to the sun. The sun goes to bed.
- **Fear (17–31 s).** The blue dark washes across the page. Tuck pulls into his shell. Silence. Inside, it is very dark.
- **Turn (31–44 s).** *Tap, tap.* A tiny glow: Firefly. "Come with me." Tuck peeks out and takes one little step.
- **Wonder (44–54 s).** A tight follow along the pond: everything Firefly touches lights up; more fireflies arrive; the dark twinkles.
- **Ending (54–60 s).** Tuck curls up, smiling. Firefly settles on his shell like a night-light. Push in on his sleeping face. "Goodnight, little turtle."

## 3. Benchmark

- **The Gruffalo (2009 TV short)**, picture-book adaptation for pre-schoolers. *Learn:* unhurried pacing, a narrator who leaves room, wide establishing shots that settle before each line, the warmth of a single small hero against a big world. *Don't take:* characters, rhymes, music, designs.
- **Bedtime picture books read aloud on pre-school TV.** *Learn:* one idea per page, line length a three-year-old can hold, repetition (wave to the sun / wave to the moon). *Don't take:* any text or famous bedtime phrases.

## 4. Characters and crayon box

- **Tuck**: a small round turtle, ≈2.5 heads tall on his feet. Leaf-green head and legs, olive shell with sand-coloured hexagon patches, dot eyes with a white catchlight, pink cheeks. He acts with big poses: play (one leg up), wave, yawn, hide (head and legs pulled in, two eyes peeking from the gap), peek, look up, sleepy smile.
- **Firefly**: a tiny lemon body with a glowing tail, two scribbled wings, big kind eyes. The glow is lemon crayon on wax white, so it shines through the wash.
- **Sun** and **Moon** with faces (child logic). The moon is drawn in white wax: invisible until the dark arrives.
- **Box (11 crayons):** indigo (all outlines, never black), leaf green, olive, sand, lemon, orange, sky blue, pink, reed green, brown, **white** (wax resist). **One watercolour:** ultramarine, for the night. It never paints Tuck or Firefly.

## 5. Shot list

The page is a long panorama (world ≈ 5200×1800): the sunny pond on the left, the reeds and the lily pads to the right. The camera travels left to right with the story.

| # | Time | Framing / angle | Camera | Content | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.0–5.0 | Extreme close-up → full | **Pull out** from a crayon tip mid-stroke (leaf green) | Tuck's last outline being drawn; the pond appears around him | Hook: the book is drawing itself |
| 2 | 5.0–11.0 | Full shot | Slow **truck** right with Tuck | Tuck plays at the water's edge: steps, a splash, a flower | Meet the hero; the sunny world |
| 3 | 11.0–17.0 | Medium-wide | **Tilt up** along his gaze to the sun, then back down | Tuck waves to the sun; it waves back, yawns and slides behind the hill | Sets up the goodbye; child logic |
| 4 | 17.0–24.0 | Wide, locked | **Held wide while the brush moves** | The ultramarine wash sweeps across; Tuck looks around, worried, pulls into his shell | Night falling *is* the wash; fear rises with it |
| 5 | 24.0–31.0 | Close on the shell | **Push in**, slow | Just the shell, two eyes in the dark gap. Boil calms to half. Near silence | The breath before the turn |
| 6 | 31.0–38.0 | Close two-shot | Locked, small drift | *Tap, tap.* Firefly lands, glowing; "Come with me." | The turn; small and gentle |
| 7 | 38.0–44.0 | Medium | Locked → starts to follow | Tuck's head and legs come out; one little step | Anticipation; bravery in a small act |
| 8 | 44.0–54.0 | Medium-wide | **Tight follow** (truck right) | Firefly flies ahead; a flower, a mushroom, a frog's eyes, lily pads light up one by one, drawn live in lemon/orange; more fireflies join | Signature shot: the world lights up ahead of the hero |
| 9 | 54.0–60.0 | Medium → close | **Push in** on Tuck's face | Tuck curls up on a lily pad, Firefly on his shell; he falls asleep smiling | Ending on the character, warm and small |

Camera moves: pull out, truck, tilt, held wide, push in, locked, tight follow (7). Transitions inside the medium only: drawing on, the wash, camera moves.

## 7. Cue map

- **Tempo 84 bpm, 4/4** (one bar = 2.857 s); 21 bars = 60 s. Cuts and key actions land on bar or half-bar lines.
- **Bars 1–6 (0–17 s), day:** ukulele lilt in G major (plucked, modelled), recorder plays the theme once, shaker on the off-beats, a soft woodblock on Tuck's steps.
- **Bars 7–10 (17–28.5 s), dark arriving:** the ukulele thins to single notes, a low bassoon/clarinet holds, the shaker stops. Bar 9 drops to near silence (faint crickets).
- **Bar 11 (28.5–31.4 s):** silence except the narrator. The first sound after it is the **tap, tap** (woodblock).
- **Bars 12–15 (31.4–42.8 s), the turn:** a single toy-piano note for Firefly's glow; the ukulele comes back softly, slower.
- **Bars 16–19 (42.8–54.2 s), the walk of lights:** the theme in full for the first time, ukulele and recorder; a toy-piano note on each light as it is drawn (rising), glockenspiel sparkles as the fireflies arrive.
- **Bars 20–21 (54.2–60 s), goodnight:** everything falls away to the recorder alone, holding the last note of the theme as Tuck falls asleep.

## 9. Subtitles and titles

- The printed words of the book: a handwriting face (Patrick Hand, OFL), 54 px, indigo, bottom-centre on a ragged patch of bare paper, written letter by letter (~0.35 s), each glyph slightly tilted, boiling on twos. Held ≥ max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). None during the wordless star passage.
- Where the wash covers the bottom of the page, the text patch is bare paper kept clear of the wash, so the words always sit on paper.
- Title: hand-lettered "Tuck and the Dark" in the last 2 s? No: the film opens on a story already being drawn, and a title would slow the hook. The title is written on the book's cover in the final pull-out, letter by letter, held ≥ 4 s if the timing allows, otherwise left out.
